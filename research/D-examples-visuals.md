# D. Examples and visuals

## D2. Styling mockups

Model (decided 2026-11-02, see [C-statistics.md](C-statistics.md) decision 9): two levels, **Paved** and **Unpaved**, plus an optional **Off-road mark** drawn on top of **Unpaved roads only** when there is positive evidence (`smoothness`, `tracktype`, `4wd_only`). A paved road never gets the mark ("Paved + Off-road makes no sense"). A provider can ship the two levels first and add the mark later.

Generated by [mockups/make-mockups.ts](mockups/make-mockups.ts) (`node mockups/make-mockups.ts`, SVG), PNG made with headless Chrome at 2x. Straight roads in the four OSM Carto class colours (red, orange, yellow, white), each in three states: Paved, Unpaved, Unpaved + Off-road; a second set at a narrow width (about z12-13). Off-road always looks rougher than Unpaved, and the road always keeps its class colour. The default is picked after the demo and the screenshot loop (todo.md decision 5). All style values live in one place, `SPEC` in make-mockups.ts, which also writes [mockups/styles.json](mockups/styles.json) for the demo.

Review (2026-11-09), styles numbered by preference (ranking 2026-11-09: centre line, dashed casing, grain, dashes):

1. **Centre line**: the clearest on every colour and at both widths; the class colour stays. Lighter brown and 1 px on narrow roads, so it does not read as a third dark line or take over the road. In OSM Carto track rhythms (long dashes Unpaved, medium dashes Off-road): the line stays inside the casing, so it does not read as a track beside the road (see the track row of the references).
2. **Dashed casing** (replaced open gaps on 2026-11-09): the fill stays continuous and only the outline is dashed, as Gaia GPS draws tracks and DMD2 draws unpaved roads, so two apps already use it. Open gaps broke the road into pieces, which imagico warns makes roads hard to follow (D3). Styles 1, 2 and 4 share one track rhythm (`RHYTHM` in make-mockups.ts), taken from OSM Carto's track grades at about 1.36 x: long dashes 15/6 for Unpaved (`grade2`, 11/4), medium dashes 9/7 for Off-road (`grade3`, 7/5); style 4 (dashes) at 1.8 x. Rougher = more broken, as in Carto. The dash-dot was dropped (2026-11-09): in Carto it marks a track with no `tracktype`, so it means unknown, not rough. The dashed outline is darker on red and orange (the normal casing is too close to the fill) and on white. Off-road history: dash-dot dropped (means unknown `tracktype` in Carto), then `grade4` short sparse dashes dropped (read as dots); medium dashes (`grade3`) chosen by Julien on 2026-11-09 as the ladder that tracks will reuse later. Not dense short dashes: a dense short-dashed casing (4/2) is how OSM Carto and most styles draw tunnels; check the Off-road outline next to tunnels in the demo. Like the centre line, meant for renderers other than OSM Carto.
3. **Grain**: based on the OSM Carto pattern, the only style already live (OSM Carto, OsmAnd web map). Carto's own grain is too faint, so it is drawn 1.5 x larger on a fill tinted a little toward brown; white roads stay white with warm brown dots.
4. **Dashes**: very clear on coloured roads. Light brown instead of grey on white roads, which otherwise look like Carto construction. Uses the shared track rhythm (below), 1.8 x larger because it fills the whole road. Was reversed for a while on 2026-11-09 (dark tint in the gaps, so Off-road looked darker); undone the same day so all line styles follow one rule, more broken = rougher, not darker = rougher.

Dropped: the brief's brown fill (the class shows only in the casing) and tint (too faint; a stronger tint makes yellow look orange, so it changes the class you see). Checked against Carto construction, tunnel and track in [mockups/references.png](mockups/references.png).

Design history: the earlier options Tint + dash, Dashes + dots and Dots + dense dots (review 2026-11-02: Tint + dash was the clearest) were replaced by the five styles of the brief on 2026-11-09, then reduced to four. Dots + dash (regular dots, brown centre dash) was dropped on 2026-11-09: busy when both are on, dots and dash compete inside a narrow road. The first mockups (three levels Paved / Rough / Off-road, options texture, centre dash, tinted fill) were replaced when the model changed to two levels plus a mark. Earlier feedback kept: regular dots, not random speckles; one grain size, only spacing changes; light tint so the road still reads as its class colour, not as a brown track.

## D3. Design research: imagico's road articles and OSM Carto markings (2026-11-09)

Christoph Hormann (imagico) designed the unpaved grain used by OSM Carto and wrote about road styling in OSM-based maps. Read: [Navigating the Maze, part 2](https://imagico.de/blog/en/navigating-the-maze-part-2/) (2021, section on unpaved roads), [Navigating the Maze, part 1](https://imagico.de/blog/en/navigating-the-maze-part-1/) (2021, road layering only), [Drawing the lines](https://imagico.de/blog/en/drawing-the-lines/) (2017, dashing of tracks and paths), [Rethinking road access restrictions rendering](https://imagico.de/blog/en/rethinking-road-access-restrictions-rendering/) (2021). Not read yet: [Drawing the line, on the use of line signatures in maps](https://imagico.de/blog/en/drawing-the-line-on-the-use-of-line-signatures-in-maps/) (2023).

What he says, and what it means for our styles:

1. **Grain is his preferred signal**: a fine-grained pattern in the road fill is "highly intuitive in its meaning and can be tuned to be fairly subtle". He tried dashing first and dropped it. Supports style 3 (grain), which is already the OSM Carto direction. Credit him and Lukas Sommer (the 2D fill pattern, OSM Carto PR 3399) in the article.
2. **Dashes on main roads collide with existing markings**: in OSM Carto, tunnels have a dashed casing, construction a dashed fill, and restricted access marks along the middle of the road. He found surface dashing "very noisy" or too close to these. Our references cover tunnels and construction (D2); access restrictions are the third case (below).
3. **Dashed lines break continuity**: a dashed or dotted line makes it harder to follow a road as one line. A point against style 4 (dashes) to weigh in the default-style decision, and the reason open gaps were replaced by a dashed casing (style 2): the fill stays continuous in styles 1, 2 and 3.
4. **Contrast per road colour**: he tuned his access dashing per fill colour. We already do this (per-class grain colours and dash tints).
5. **Patchy tagging becomes visible noise**: a mark that appears only where tags exist looks random. Fits the Off-road evidence finding (5.8% of Unpaved km, [stats/mark-coverage.md](stats/mark-coverage.md)): keep the mark optional.
6. **Readable without a key**: his test for a general map. The two base levels should pass it; the Off-road mark may need the legend.
7. Nothing in these articles on "missing surface means paved".

**OSM Carto access marking** (checked in `style/roads.mss`, [commit 003cefdd](https://github.com/openstreetmap-carto/openstreetmap-carto/blob/003cefdd/style/roads.mss)): restricted roads (`access=private`, `no` and similar for the road's main mode) get a row of light grey round dots along the middle of the fill from z15 (`line-dasharray: 0.1,9`, 3 px, round caps; 6 px at z17), on every class from motorway to residential. Carto already combines it with the unpaved grain (`@access-marking-white-unpaved`).

**Style 1 (centre line) is not meant for OSM Carto.** Its line sits where Carto puts the access dots, and Carto already has the grain. The centre line is for other renderers that have no marking in the middle of the road and prefer a line signal over a pattern (for example vector styles where 2D fill patterns are hard to draw). The Carto-related risks (access dots, tracks next to roads) still matter for readers who know the Carto look, so they stay in the references.

Open question (2026-11-09): Unpaved centre line as long dashes (Carto `tracktype=grade2` rhythm, current, shared with the dashed casing of style 2) or solid. Long dashes look almost solid at z13-14 and a dashed middle line is the pattern imagico warns about; solid keeps the road one continuous line and still differs from the Off-road medium dashes. To decide in the demo.

## D1b. Street-level imagery check

Satellite alone is not enough evidence, so each example needs Mapillary or Google Street View. [examples/imagery-check.ts](examples/imagery-check.ts) samples points along each way (8 in round 1, 4 in later screenings) and looks for the nearest image within 50 m, with the same lookups as `unpaved-app/app/js/street-view.js`. Keys are read from `unpaved-app` at run time, never stored here. `GOOGLE=0` skips Google entirely (Google Static images cost money; metadata calls are free). Candidates: [examples/candidates.txt](examples/candidates.txt) (wiki and famous roads) plus the longest Unpaved major roads per country from the stats. Full table: [examples/imagery.md](examples/imagery.md).

Rounds:

1. 2026-11-02: 62 roads (first 18 countries), Google and Mapillary, 15 reviewed.
2. 2026-11-02: 198 more roads, 4 points each, 14 reviewed with Mapillary only.
3. 2026-11-03: 304 roads in the 24 late-added rows, Mapillary only, 26 reviewed.

No coverage at all on the checked roads: Gabon RN4 and RN8, Bolivia RN39, Cameroon P12 and D21, Laos, Myanmar, Gunbarrel Highway, Oodnadatta Track, Tanami Road, the Australian guideline examples, Sri Lanka, Honduras, Albania, Iran, Solomon Islands, Antarctica. These stay statistics only.
