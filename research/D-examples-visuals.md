# D. Examples and visuals

## D2. Styling mockups

Model (decided 2026-11-02, see [C-statistics.md](C-statistics.md) decision 9): two levels, **Paved** and **Unpaved**, plus an optional **Off-road mark** drawn on top of **Unpaved roads only** when there is positive evidence (`smoothness`, `tracktype`, `4wd_only`). A paved road never gets the mark ("Paved + Off-road makes no sense"). A provider can ship the two levels first and add the mark later.

Generated by [mockups/make-mockups.ts](mockups/make-mockups.ts) (`node mockups/make-mockups.ts`, SVG), PNG made with headless Chrome at 2x. Straight roads in the four OSM Carto class colours (red, orange, yellow, white), each in three states: Paved, Unpaved, Unpaved + Off-road; a second set at a narrow width (about z12-13). Off-road always looks rougher than Unpaved, and the road always keeps its class colour. The four styles go from faint to strong and none is preferred: each provider picks the one that fits its map (todo.md decision 5). All style values live in one place, `SPEC` in make-mockups.ts, which also writes [mockups/styles.json](mockups/styles.json) for the demo.

Review (2026-11-09, reordered 2026-11-10 from faint to strong, no preference: the right style depends on the provider):

1. **Grain fill**: based on the OSM Carto pattern, the only style already live (OSM Carto, OsmAnd web map). Carto's own grain is too faint, so it is drawn 1.5 x larger on a fill tinted a little toward brown; white roads stay white with warm brown dots.
2. **Dashed centre line**: the clearest on every colour and at both widths; the class colour stays. Lighter brown and 1 px on narrow roads, so it does not read as a third dark line or take over the road. In OSM Carto track rhythms (long dashes Unpaved, medium dashes Off-road): the line stays inside the casing, so it does not read as a track beside the road (see the track row of the references).
3. **Dashed fill**: very clear on coloured roads. Dashes in a centred band 60% of the road width (full width below 6 px), long dashes (shared rhythm 1.8 x), so a thin edge of the normal fill stays along both sides. Chosen by Julien on 2026-11-10 over full width (closer to Carto construction) and over a 75% band with shorter dashes. Unpaved dashes at a luminance contrast of 1.3 against the fill (1.4 on red and orange, raised 2026-11-10: they looked soft); in the tint version the Off-road dashes are 1.35 darker again. Unpaved dashes raised to a luminance contrast of 1.3 against the fill (they were 1.24 on red and orange); in the tint version the Off-road dashes are 1.35 darker again. DMD2 draws unpaved roads this way (dark dashes in the class-coloured fill), so one app already uses it. Light brown instead of grey on white roads, which otherwise look like Carto construction. Uses the shared track rhythm (below), 1.8 x larger because it fills the whole road. Was reversed for a while on 2026-11-09 (dark tint in the gaps, so Off-road looked darker); undone the same day so all line styles follow one rule, more broken = rougher, not darker = rougher.
4. **Dashed casing** (replaced open gaps on 2026-11-09): the fill stays continuous and only the outline is dashed. The dashed outline is the one Gaia GPS uses for tracks, and for roads it draws as tracks (D3); here the class colour is kept. Open gaps broke the road into pieces, which imagico warns makes roads hard to follow (D3). The dashed centre line, dashed fill and dashed casing share one track rhythm (`RHYTHM` in make-mockups.ts), in the order of OSM Carto's track grades (`grade2` longer than `grade3`) but with a bigger step between them, so the two are easy to tell apart (widened on 2026-11-09 after 15/6 vs 9/7 looked too close): long dashes and short gaps 22/6 for Unpaved, short dashes and gaps a bit longer than the dashes 8/9 for Off-road; the dashed fill at 1.8 x. Limits: Off-road dashes 7 px or more (shorter read as dots), Unpaved gaps 5 px or more (shorter look solid at z13-14). Rougher = more broken, as in Carto. The dash-dot was dropped (2026-11-09): in Carto it marks a track with no `tracktype`, so it means unknown, not rough. The dashed outline is darker on red and orange (the normal casing is too close to the fill) and on white. Off-road history: dash-dot dropped (means unknown `tracktype` in Carto), then `grade4` short sparse dashes dropped (read as dots); medium dashes (`grade3`) chosen by Julien on 2026-11-09 as the ladder that tracks will reuse later. Not dense short dashes: a dense short-dashed casing (4/2) is how OSM Carto and most styles draw tunnels; check the Off-road outline next to tunnels in the demo. Like the centre line, meant for renderers other than OSM Carto.
5. **Dashed fill, full width** (added 2026-11-10): the dashed fill over the whole road width, as Americana draws unpaved roads (and DMD2), so it is the smallest change for Americana and the OpenMapTiles-based maps. Kept next to the 60% band version (style 3), which is softer. Its Unpaved dash is lighter than style 3's (contrast 1.25 against the fill; white keeps the tan dash so it does not fade into the land), since full width already covers most of the road. Style 3 needs no minimum dash length: below 6 px the plain tint takes over, and at 6 px its dashes are still about 22 px long.

**Off-road, official since 2026-11-10: the Unpaved pattern, only darker** (Julien's choice over "more broken", which is kept as a backup in `options-comparison-broken`). Reasons: the colour survives at zoom 12-14 where dashes merge, no style looks like a Carto tunnel any more, and one rule for all five styles. The dash ladder (decision 9 in todo.md) stays for tracks later.

**Low zoom (2026-11-10): below 6 px of road width every style is a plain tint**, since grain, thin lines and casing dashes blur there: `lowTint` for Unpaved, `offTint` for Off-road. Tints measured as steps of luminance contrast of 1.2 or more (fill to `lowTint` to `offTint`), `offTint` also 1.35 or more against the fill, and against the land 1.3 or more (1.5 for white, tan). Values: red `#ed9d84` / `#e98667`, orange `#eec28b` / `#e8ac61`, yellow `#e3e798` / `#d0d651`, white `#e2cfb8` / `#d6bc9b`. The grain dots are darker on yellow and white (`#3f4318`, `#46362a`), where the grain was faint.

Dropped: the brief's brown fill (the class shows only in the casing) and tint (too faint; a stronger tint makes yellow look orange, so it changes the class you see). Checked against Carto construction, tunnel and track in [mockups/references.png](mockups/references.png).

Design history: the earlier options Tint + dash, Dashes + dots and Dots + dense dots (review 2026-11-02: Tint + dash was the clearest) were replaced by the five styles of the brief on 2026-11-09, then reduced to four. Dots + dash (regular dots, brown centre dash) was dropped on 2026-11-09: busy when both are on, dots and dash compete inside a narrow road. The first mockups (three levels Paved / Rough / Off-road, options texture, centre dash, tinted fill) were replaced when the model changed to two levels plus a mark. Earlier feedback kept: regular dots, not random speckles; one grain size, only spacing changes; light tint so the road still reads as its class colour, not as a brown track.

## D3. Design research: imagico's road articles and OSM Carto markings (2026-11-09)

Christoph Hormann (imagico) designed the unpaved grain used by OSM Carto and wrote about road styling in OSM-based maps. Read: [Navigating the Maze, part 2](https://imagico.de/blog/en/navigating-the-maze-part-2/) (2021, section on unpaved roads), [Navigating the Maze, part 1](https://imagico.de/blog/en/navigating-the-maze-part-1/) (2021, road layering only), [Drawing the lines](https://imagico.de/blog/en/drawing-the-lines/) (2017, dashing of tracks and paths), [Rethinking road access restrictions rendering](https://imagico.de/blog/en/rethinking-road-access-restrictions-rendering/) (2021). Not read yet: [Drawing the line, on the use of line signatures in maps](https://imagico.de/blog/en/drawing-the-line-on-the-use-of-line-signatures-in-maps/) (2023).

What he says, and what it means for our styles:

1. **Grain is his preferred signal**: a fine-grained pattern in the road fill is "highly intuitive in its meaning and can be tuned to be fairly subtle". He tried dashing first and dropped it. Supports style 1 (grain fill), which is already the OSM Carto direction. Credit him and Lukas Sommer (the 2D fill pattern, OSM Carto PR 3399) in the article.
2. **Dashes on main roads collide with existing markings**: in OSM Carto, tunnels have a dashed casing, construction a dashed fill, and restricted access marks along the middle of the road. He found surface dashing "very noisy" or too close to these. Our references cover tunnels and construction (D2); access restrictions are the third case (below).
3. **Dashed lines break continuity**: a dashed or dotted line makes it harder to follow a road as one line. A point against the dashed fill (style 3) to weigh per provider, and the reason open gaps were replaced by a dashed casing (style 4): the fill stays continuous in the grain fill, the dashed centre line and the dashed casing.
4. **Contrast per road colour**: he tuned his access dashing per fill colour. We already do this (per-class grain colours and dash tints).
5. **Patchy tagging becomes visible noise**: a mark that appears only where tags exist looks random. Fits the Off-road evidence finding (5.8% of Unpaved km, [stats/mark-coverage.md](stats/mark-coverage.md)): keep the mark optional.
6. **Readable without a key**: his test for a general map. The two base levels should pass it; the Off-road mark may need the legend.
7. Nothing in these articles on "missing surface means paved".

**Gaia GPS track look: credit the dashed outline, not the class change** (2026-11-10). Gaia Topo draws roads with very bad `smoothness` like tracks: dashed casing and light fill, the class colour gone. Julien sees this on any class; at the Bolivia spot the F10 trunk stayed a normal road, so the exact rule is not confirmed (A0). In the proposal and the article, present it as:

1. **Good idea, credited**: the dashed outline is a clear, well-known way to say "not a normal road". Style 4 (dashed casing) takes it from Gaia.
2. **Bad practice, explained**: drawing a road as a track loses its class. A trunk that looks like a track no longer says it is the main route between two towns, the same problem as Ride with GPS, the OsmAnd Motorcycle styles and HOT (A0).
3. **Risk for the data**: when unpaved roads look like tracks, mappers who want the map to look right may change `highway=*` to match (tagging for the renderer), and wrong classes spread. The rendering should follow the tags, not push mappers to change them.
4. **Two questions mixed**: `highway=*` says how important the road is, `surface` and `smoothness` say what it is like to drive. One signal for both makes both harder to read.

Wording for the article: "Gaia GPS shows the right instinct, a dashed outline, but applies it by turning roads into tracks. Keep the outline, keep the class."


**OSM Carto access marking** (checked in `style/roads.mss`, [commit 003cefdd](https://github.com/openstreetmap-carto/openstreetmap-carto/blob/003cefdd/style/roads.mss)): restricted roads (`access=private`, `no` and similar for the road's main mode) get a row of light grey round dots along the middle of the fill from z15 (`line-dasharray: 0.1,9`, 3 px, round caps; 6 px at z17), on every class from motorway to residential. Carto already combines it with the unpaved grain (`@access-marking-white-unpaved`).

**Style 2 (dashed centre line) is not meant for OSM Carto.** Its line sits where Carto puts the access dots, and Carto already has the grain. The centre line is for other renderers that have no marking in the middle of the road and prefer a line signal over a pattern (for example vector styles where 2D fill patterns are hard to draw). The Carto-related risks (access dots, tracks next to roads) still matter for readers who know the Carto look, so they stay in the references.

Open question (2026-11-09): Unpaved centre line as long dashes (Carto `tracktype=grade2` rhythm, current, shared with the dashed casing of style 2) or solid. Long dashes look almost solid at z13-14 and a dashed middle line is the pattern imagico warns about; solid keeps the road one continuous line and still differs from the Off-road medium dashes. To decide in the demo.

## D1b. Street-level imagery check

Satellite alone is not enough evidence, so each example needs Mapillary or Google Street View. [examples/imagery-check.ts](examples/imagery-check.ts) samples points along each way (8 in round 1, 4 in later screenings) and looks for the nearest image within 50 m, with the same lookups as `unpaved-app/app/js/street-view.js`. Keys are read from `unpaved-app` at run time, never stored here. `GOOGLE=0` skips Google entirely (Google Static images cost money; metadata calls are free). Candidates: [examples/candidates.txt](examples/candidates.txt) (wiki and famous roads) plus the longest Unpaved major roads per country from the stats. Full table: [examples/imagery.md](examples/imagery.md).

Rounds:

1. 2026-11-02: 62 roads (first 18 countries), Google and Mapillary, 15 reviewed.
2. 2026-11-02: 198 more roads, 4 points each, 14 reviewed with Mapillary only.
3. 2026-11-03: 304 roads in the 24 late-added rows, Mapillary only, 26 reviewed.

No coverage at all on the checked roads: Gabon RN4 and RN8, Bolivia RN39, Cameroon P12 and D21, Laos, Myanmar, Gunbarrel Highway, Oodnadatta Track, Tanami Road, the Australian guideline examples, Sri Lanka, Honduras, Albania, Iran, Solomon Islands, Antarctica. These stay statistics only.
